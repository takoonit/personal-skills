# Intentional minimalism

Use with the shared foundations. Remove competing signals while concentrating identity in a few recognisable choices. Blank space alone is not a design system.

## Character and fit

Useful when visitors need focus, confidence and clear evaluation: professional services, considered purchases, portfolios and repeated work. It may feel premium through precision and content quality, but sparse layouts do not prove quality or suit every audience. Rich data and complex tasks can still use restrained presentation without hiding information.

The main risks are a generic SaaS template, weak brand memory and missing explanations. State where character lives before removing decoration: a particular type relationship, product crop, alignment, colour relation or framing device.

## Composition, colour and spacing rules

- Define one visual priority per meaningful region. On a task screen this is the active work area, not a promotional hero above it. Let supporting actions remain findable without equal visual weight.
- Use a clear alignment system and a small set of spacing roles: within control, within group, between groups and between sections. Make related items visibly closer than unrelated items. Avoid repeated giant blank gaps that disconnect the story.
- Start with a quiet canvas, readable text and a purposeful accent. Neutral does not have to mean white; derive the palette from the brand and content. Preserve separate status semantics.
- Choose one or two recognisable identity details and repeat them deliberately. Avoid adding unrelated badges, gradient blobs, floating glass cards and decorative pill labels to compensate for weak content.
- Use whitespace to separate before drawing another card. Reserve containers for grouping, interaction or surface changes. Repeated rows, rules or open composition may be clearer than a grid of identical cards.
- Choose images for specificity and relevance. A distinctive product crop or honest demonstration can carry the composition; generic lifestyle photography cannot substitute for a clear proposition.

## All six techniques

| Technique | Concrete application |
|---|---|
| Anchor typography | Assign a characterful headline voice and calm support. Contrast may come from weights/widths in one family. Set real title/body/label roles rather than making every section an oversized headline. |
| Star of the show | Choose the product, meaningful statement, image or main work area. Give it more visual priority through proportion and placement, not a mandatory full-screen hero. |
| Visual rhyming | Repeat one precise detail, such as a crop ratio, fine rule, offset alignment or shaped marker, in at least two named supporting locations. Keep recurrence purposeful and small. |
| Subtle depth | Separate relevant planes through a tonal surface, controlled overlap or restrained shadow for a true overlay. Do not introduce blur/noise just to satisfy the technique. A flat system can still clarify foreground/background relationships. |
| Text opacity and emphasis | Use primary, supporting and incidental semantic colours with verified contrast. Keep instructions, prices, errors and field labels clear; faint text is not evidence of refinement. |
| Design variations | Compare image-led asymmetry with typography-led stacking, or two arrangements of the working area. Keep content identical so the comparison tests hierarchy rather than omission. |

## Components and interaction

Keep buttons and links visibly actionable. Do not replace meaningful text labels with unfamiliar icons or remove input labels in favour of placeholders. Secondary actions may be quieter, but they still need a recognisable treatment and focus state.

Define compact and comfortable density only when actual use requires both. Reuse existing table/form/list components; avoid forcing marketing spacing into an operational view. Empty states should explain what to do, errors should offer recovery, and loading should preserve enough structure to prevent jumps. These states are part of the design, not clutter to delete.

Use motion for state continuity and feedback. Prefer a small consistent transition family. Do not animate every element on scroll, hide content behind slow reveals or delay a response to seem polished.

## Mobile, content and delivery

Collapse whitespace and recompose the focal element before reducing text size. Keep meaningful information and actions in the narrow layout. A stripped-down phone view must not become an incomplete product.

Check the layout with long titles, additional navigation items and realistic descriptions. Minimalism is fragile when it only works with one perfect sentence. Keep alternate content and asset-loading fallbacks usable. Reduced motion keeps state changes immediate and comprehensible. Optimise prominent images and fonts; sparse composition does not automatically produce a small payload.

## Failure patterns and review

| Reject | Verify instead |
|---|---|
| The brand could be replaced without changing anything else | Name and locate the recurring identity details |
| Labels or explanations vanish to make space | The primary task remains understandable on first use |
| Every section is a huge headline plus empty space | Hierarchy follows information value and reading sequence |
| All controls are low-contrast text | Action, state and focus remain discoverable |

Possible project rules: use the named image frame and asymmetric alignment for identity; use surface containers only for genuine groups; keep field labels and recovery text visible. Preserve useful density while removing competing signals.
