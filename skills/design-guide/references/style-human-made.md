# Human-made design

Use with the shared foundations. Build identity from specific people, places, materials and authored choices. A handmade-looking treatment is an aesthetic; human authorship and provenance require evidence.

## Character and fit

Useful for independent products, craft, cultural/community work and brands whose story depends on origin or people. The audience may value specificity and connection, but do not infer purchasing preference from the appearance of a texture or illustration.

The main constraint is an asset supply that can be maintained. A custom collage system may need more art direction than a photograph-led system. If documentary assets are missing, identify the gap and use an honest provisional treatment rather than inventing a maker, customer, quotation or origin story.

## Asset, composition and colour rules

- Choose a primary asset language: documentary photography, authored illustration, lettering, physical artefacts or a controlled collage. Supporting techniques may accompany it, but they need a shared line, crop, colour or material relationship.
- Document provenance and usage rights when assets are supplied or researched. Distinguish commissioned/original, licensed, generated and unknown material. Do not label generated people or workshops as documentary evidence.
- Art-direct photography through subject, distance, light, crop and context. Preserve details that communicate real use or place. Authenticity does not require deliberately poor exposure, blur or low resolution.
- For illustration, define stroke character, shape vocabulary, palette and detail level. Repeat the drawing language across icons, spot images and larger pieces; avoid mixing unrelated stock illustration packs.
- Put controlled irregularity in image edges, framing, marks or display lettering. Align the reading and task structure predictably underneath. Do not randomise rotation, spacing or brush strokes on every render.
- Sample colour relationships from the project and assets, then translate them into accessible semantic tokens. Earth tones, cream paper and muted colours are options, not proof of craft.
- Give texture a source, scale and location. Avoid tiled seams and obvious repetition; keep high-frequency texture out of text/input surfaces. Limit the number of competing material effects.

## All six techniques

| Technique | Concrete application |
|---|---|
| Anchor typography | Use a distinctive display/lettering voice with dependable reading type. Keep prices, form labels and instructions in the readable system. Check required scripts rather than imitating a Latin handwritten face with an unrelated Thai fallback. |
| Star of the show | Lead with a specific photograph, illustration, artefact or person/story relevant to the action. Record what it demonstrates, its source and the important part that must survive cropping. |
| Visual rhyming | Repeat a drawing gesture, cut edge, caption frame or material mark in at least two named places. Specify its stable rules so the interface feels authored rather than randomly distressed. |
| Subtle depth | Use a restrained sourced texture, ink/paper relation or layered photo edge. Keep material treatment subordinate to the focal subject and preserve clean reading surfaces. |
| Text opacity and emphasis | Define emphasis on stable areas; lower supporting-text emphasis must survive the texture and image crop. Put captions on a backing or outside the image when contrast is uncertain. |
| Design variations | Compare photo-led presentation with illustration/collage-led presentation of the same story. Judge credibility, asset availability, maintainability and task clarity. Treat unavailable assets as constraints, not promises. |

## Components and content operations

Keep controls precise even when surrounding artwork is irregular. A rough outline can decorate a real button; the hit area, label, focus ring and disabled state remain consistent. Custom lettering should not be the only readable text for essential actions. Use an accessible equivalent for meaningful image lettering.

Define repeatable crops and image roles for new content, plus fallback rules for missing images. Include credit/caption placement where appropriate. Avoid hardcoding new page layouts around the exact shape of one current photograph. Reuse existing upload/content conventions; do not create an asset-management system as a side effect of writing design guidance.

Keep identity-bearing imperfections intentional. A hand-drawn underline used for emphasis should not appear on every heading, price and link with different meanings. Status/error icons still require consistent semantics.

## Mobile, motion and delivery

Choose focal points for narrow crops and preserve faces, objects and contextual evidence. Recompose a collage into a readable sequence; avoid shrinking all pieces or hiding essential context behind overlap. Images and texture overlays must not intercept taps.

Motion may reveal layers or respond gently to a meaningful action; endless wobbling does not make artwork feel more human. Reduced motion keeps the authored composition intact. Optimise large scans/photos and avoid full-page high-resolution textures when a small bounded asset suffices. Provide honest alternatives for unavailable documentary assets.

## Failure patterns and review

| Reject | Verify instead |
|---|---|
| A stock or generated portrait is presented as the maker | Authorship/provenance labels match available evidence |
| Every element is distressed or crooked | Consistent reading/task structure supports selective irregularity |
| Collage hides product facts or required action | Focal crop, copy and navigation work on narrow screens |
| One beautiful page cannot accept new content | Repeatable asset roles and fallbacks cover ordinary updates |

Possible project rules: use supplied workshop photography with documented crop priorities; reserve rough edges for media; keep controls and reading surfaces clean. Choose only rules supported by the actual content and assets.
