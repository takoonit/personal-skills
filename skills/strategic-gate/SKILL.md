# Strategic Gate

Decide whether to pursue, defer, replace, or reject proposed work. Finish with a defensible priority and the evidence that would reopen it.

## Decision rules

- Separate observed facts, assumptions, and consequential unknowns. Repetition does not turn an assumption into evidence.
- Prefer the smallest commitment that achieves the outcome or resolves the weakest important assumption.
- Compare opportunity cost, reversibility, present benefit, and maintenance burden. Novelty and technical elegance need a concrete benefit.
- Reuse accepted decisions. Reopen one only when new evidence changes its premise.

## Approach

1. Establish the intended outcome, beneficiary, constraints, and reason to act now from available context. Ask only for information that could change the decision.
2. Identify the weakest consequential assumption and a cheap test that could disprove it.
3. When alternatives matter, compare the baseline with the recommended move. Add a third option only if it changes the choice.
4. Challenge the recommendation against displaced work, a cheaper path, and evidence that would make it wrong.

Choose one **Now** priority when prioritizing competing initiatives. It may be an experiment, a deliberate hold, or stopping the work. Include **Next** and **Later** only when sequencing helps; do not invent follow-on work to fill a format.

## Finish

Return a verdict (**Now**, **Defer**, **Replace**, or **Reject**), the decisive evidence and uncertainty, and the next action or stopping condition. For accepted work, include its observable success condition, constraints, and revisit trigger.

A prioritization request ends with that decision. If the user also authorized execution, continue within the accepted scope. Suggest a specialist only for a remaining need: commercial viability belongs to `shark-tank`; unresolved product or architecture direction belongs to `shape-system-work`. A hand-off never grants new authority.
