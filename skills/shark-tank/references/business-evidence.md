# Business evidence

Read when the pitch lacks founder-specific facts or current market evidence could change the verdict.

## Interview selectively

Reuse answers already supplied. Ask a small batch only when needed:

- Who has the problem, and what do they do today?
- What has been observed, tested, built, or sold? What happened?
- Who pays, for what, and at what tested price?
- Why are current substitutes insufficient?
- What time, capital, access, expertise, or audience is available?
- Is success a side business, sustainable company, venture-scale business, or strategic product?

Accept unknown answers. Distinguish what research can resolve from what only customer behavior can establish. Stop interviewing when more answers are unlikely to change the immediate decision.

## Research to challenge the thesis

Check competitors and substitutes, pricing, complaints, distribution difficulty, and relevant platform or regulatory constraints. Prefer current primary evidence and independent corroboration. Test claims such as "no competitors" directly.

Record sources and dates for facts that affect the recommendation. Market-size reports, estimated private revenue, survey intent, and recycled statistics are weak evidence unless supported. Expose conflicting estimates.

## Choose a decisive test

| Uncertainty | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | Observation of the target user's existing behavior |
| Willingness to pay | A real offer and observed purchase behavior |
| Acquisition | A bounded test of one credible channel |
| Retention | Return behavior after initial use |
| Economics | Actual delivery cost and contribution |
| Differentiation | Direct comparison with the strongest substitute |

Recommend a bounded commitment with an observable success or failure condition. Execution still needs authorization for spending, customer contact, or other external actions.

## Calibrate judgment

An idea may warrant **Prove** when demand is unclear. A plausible need with an untested offer may warrant **Validate**. Use **Invest** when evidence supports the next commitment and **Scale** when repeatable commercial behavior and economics support expansion.

A three-perspective assessment need not contain disagreement. State the binding constraint plainly: real demand can coexist with acquisition costs that make the business unattractive. **Pass** is valid even for an appealing product.
